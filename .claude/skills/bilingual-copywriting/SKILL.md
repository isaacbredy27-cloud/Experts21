# Bilingual copywriting

## Rules
- **Write natively in both languages.** Don't translate literally. Use Miami Spanish, which is Cuban-influenced and pan-Latin, and keep it natural: *cafecito, colada, cortadito, café con leche, pastelito*.
- **Coffee terms:** keep established terms (espresso, latte, cold brew) and add Spanish descriptors where they help.
- **Consistent order:** English first and Spanish second (or side by side) on menus and signs, unless the site is predominantly Spanish-speaking (for example Hialeah or Little Havana), where Spanish goes first.
- **Portuguese:** add it for tourist-facing signage in Miami Beach, Brickell, and Wynwood (Brazil is the #2 international market).
- **Tone:** follow the brand voice guide. Warm, quick, confident. Avoid jargon that sounds snobbish.
- **Legal and HR content** (job ads, policies): accurate translation. Flag anything needing legal review.

## Steps
1. Confirm the audience, channel, and length limits.
2. Draft in both languages and adapt idioms.
3. Self-check: accuracy, tone, prices and units the same in both versions, no false friends.

## Output
EN and ES versions side by side (plus PT if requested), with notes on any intentional differences.
