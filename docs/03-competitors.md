# 03 — Competitors

Miami's coffee market has **four competitive layers**. A new specialty café competes with all four for the daily coffee visit.

## 1. Local specialty roasters and cafés (direct competitors)

| Name | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Panther Coffee** | Wynwood flagship (2390 NW 2nd Ave), Miami Beach (The Betsy), others; also Traverse City, MI | Miami's specialty pioneer (roasting on site since 2010). Small-batch roaster, strong brand, also does wholesale. The benchmark for quality. |
| **Per'La Specialty Roasters** | Local roaster | Supplies many independent cafés (Infatuation notes cafés "using locally roasted beans from Per'la"). Possible supplier or wholesale partner as well as a competitor. |
| **Vice City Bean** | Miami | Cited with OTL as a top serious-espresso shop. |
| **OTL** | Miami | Café and espresso, popular with the creative crowd. |
| **Raccoon Coffee & Kitchen** | Brickell (609 Brickell Ave, waterfront), Edgewater, Wynwood | Specialty coffee plus all-day breakfast, açaí, and pastries. Pet-friendly, Wi-Fi. A multi-unit local operator. |
| **Puroast Coffee** | Brickell (632 S Miami Ave) | "Low-acid" positioning ("Feel Better, Not Bitter"). |
| Vietnamese coffee truck | Wynwood | Small-format and mobile. Shows that low-capex formats work in Wynwood. |

## 4. Cuban cafeterías and ventanitas (indirect but very important)

- Examples: **Versailles** (colada about $1.44), **Suite Habana Café** (Wynwood), **GROU** (Coral Gables: colada $3.29, cortado $4.39), plus thousands of neighborhood ventanitas.
- **Strengths:** price, speed, cultural ritual, ubiquity.
- **Weaknesses:** commodity beans, limited seating, little quality storytelling.
- **Implication:** a specialty café can't ignore Cuban coffee. It needs a credible, better version at a fair premium.

## Positioning gaps (opportunities)
1. **Specialty Cuban coffee.** Coladas and cortaditos made with traceable Latin American single origins, priced between ventanita and specialty.
2. **Speed plus quality** for Brickell and Downtown commuters, an area dominated by chains and slow third-wave cafés.
3. **Underserved neighborhoods.** Coral Way, Little River, MiMo/Biscayne Corridor, Allapattah, and Westchester have lower rents and fewer specialty options than Wynwood or Brickell.
4. **Latin American origin storytelling** that connects with Colombian, Brazilian, Venezuelan, and Central American customers. This audience is both local and tourist.

## Competitive threats
- **Wynwood saturation.** Many cafés within a few blocks, and Wynwood/Design District vacancy is 8.2%, showing turnover.
- **Luxury entrants** (Sant Ambroeus) and **experiential nationals** (La Colombe) setting a high bar for the space.
- **Chains** taking the convenience occasion with apps and drive-thrus.
