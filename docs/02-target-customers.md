Nationally, **25–39-year-olds** are the heaviest specialty coffee drinkers (64% drank it in the past week). **18–24-year-olds** account for about 32% of specialty revenue and lean toward customized, indulgent drinks.

## Priority segments

### 1. Young urban professionals (primary)
- **Where:** Brickell, Downtown, Edgewater, Wynwood, Midtown.
- **Profile:** 25–40, high income, often bilingual, many newcomers from NYC, Latin America, and Europe. Busy weekday mornings.
- **Wants:** speed in the morning rush, mobile ordering, consistent quality, iced espresso drinks, alt-milks, and a workable laptop space at off-peak times.
- **Spend:** $6–9 drink plus an occasional pastry; visits 3–5 times a week if the café is on their route.

### 2. Local Latin/Cuban-heritage daily coffee drinkers (volume and loyalty)
- **Where:** everywhere, but especially Little Havana, Coral Way, Westchester, Hialeah, and Coral Gables.
- **Profile:** multi-generational, with deep coffee habits. Very price-aware about the cafecito but willing to pay for quality and a social experience.
- **Wants:** strong, sweet, fast coffee; a Spanish-speaking team; the ritual of sharing a colada; fair prices.
- **Opportunity:** specialty-grade Cuban-style drinks, sold at a modest premium over the ventanita.

### 3. Remote and hybrid workers, and students
- **Where:** Wynwood, Little River, MiMo, Coconut Grove, near UM (Coral Gables) and FIU.
- **Wants:** Wi-Fi, outlets, air conditioning, long hours.
- **Risk:** low spend per seat-hour. Manage with power-outlet zoning, time-based seating, and add-on food.

### 4. Tourists and visitors
- **Where:** Miami Beach, Wynwood, Design District, Brickell hotels.
- **Profile:** many Latin American and European visitors with strong espresso expectations, plus U.S. domestic travelers looking for "Instagrammable" spots.
- **Wants:** a memorable product, a photogenic space, packaged beans or merch as souvenirs.
- **Risk:** seasonal (Dec–Apr peak, July–Sept trough) and not repeat customers.

### 5. Office, catering, and wholesale (B2B, secondary revenue)
- Office boxes and catering for Brickell and Downtown firms, coffee for hotel lobbies, and selling beans to restaurants. This smooths out seasonality.

## Dayparts (expected pattern, *est.*)
- **7–10am:** 40–50% of revenue. Commuters, so speed is everything.
- **10am–2pm:** remote workers, meetings, and brunch (weekends are very strong in Miami).
- **2–6pm:** iced drinks, the afternoon "colada break," and students.
- **Evenings:** only in nightlife areas, or with a wine, beer, or alcohol program (needs a separate license, see [06](06-permits-licenses.md)).

## Customer requirements checklist
- [ ] Bilingual English/Spanish menu and staff.
- [ ] Cold/iced-first menu and strong air conditioning (summer heat index is often 100°F+).
- [ ] Mobile ordering and pickup shelf for the commuter segment.
- [ ] Clear value tier, such as a specialty cafecito, alongside premium signature drinks.
- [ ] Shaded or covered outdoor seating where possible.
- [ ] Packaged beans and merch for tourists and gifting.

## Open questions
- Which single segment is primary for the first location? That choice drives the site.
- Run a customer intercept survey and social-listening scan on Miami coffee pain points (price, speed, seating, parking).
