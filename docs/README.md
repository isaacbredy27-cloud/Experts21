# Miami Specialty Coffee Shop — Research Pack

Background research for opening an independent **specialty coffee shop in Miami, Florida**. It is written as shared context for the team of expert agents that will be built on top of it, so each file stands on its own and is organized by topic.

- **Research date:** November 20, 2026
- **Scope:** City of Miami and the surrounding Miami-Dade County, with notes on Miami Beach and Coral Gables where rules differ.
- **Status:** Desk research only (public web sources). No broker quotes, landlord talks, or calls with the city have happened yet. Treat every dollar figure as a planning range to check, not a quote.

## Key findings (TL;DR)

1. **Demand is strong and growing.** U.S. specialty coffee drinking hit a 14-year high in 2025. Miami-Dade has about 2.7M residents and 28.35M visitors a year (2025). The city already has a deep coffee habit through Cuban *cafecito* culture.
2. **Rent is the biggest threat to the numbers.** Average retail asking rents (Q2 2025) run about **$89/sf in Brickell**, **$100/sf in Miami Beach**, and **$71/sf in Wynwood/Design District**, against about **$47–50/sf** on the Biscayne Corridor and Coral Way. Vacancy is tight (about 3.1% overall). Choosing the site is the most important decision.
3. **Labor costs just went up.** Florida's minimum wage reached **$15.00/hr on Sept 30, 2026** ($11.98 for tipped workers) and will rise with inflation from 2027 on. Miami barista pay runs about **$15–23/hr**.
4. **Startup budget:** plan on **about $265k–$500k** for a 1,000–1,200 sf café in a space that was already a restaurant or café ("second-generation" space). Plan on **$450k–$800k** to convert raw retail space in a top submarket. Include 4–6 months of working capital.
5. **Green coffee costs are volatile but easing.** Arabica futures hit record highs in 2025, partly because of tariffs on Brazilian coffee that were lifted in Nov 2025. They traded around **$2.70/lb** in late 2026, down about 32% from a year earlier. Prices should still be planned with volatility in mind.
6. **Positioning gap:** There are two tiers, cheap fast Cuban coffee ($1.50–$3.50) and expensive specialty "aesthetic" cafés ($6–8+ lattes). That leaves room for a concept that is serious about quality, bilingual, and fast enough for Miami's commuter rhythm. It could also use Latin American origins and Cuban-style drinks made with specialty beans.
7. **Top risks:** occupancy cost, permitting and buildout delays (often 4–9 months), hurricane and insurance exposure, summer seasonality, labor cost and turnover, and saturation in trendy neighborhoods.
