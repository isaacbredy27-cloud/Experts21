## National trends that matter for a new café

- **Premiumization.** Customers pay for origin transparency, quality, and sourcing stories. Specialty coffee carries a 20–50% premium over commodity coffee.
- **Cold and iced drinks.** Cold brew, nitro, and iced espresso drinks are year-round staples. In Miami's climate, iced drinks can be the majority of the mix (*est.*).
- **Gen Z customization.** Younger customers prefer indulgent, customizable drinks (flavored lattes, matcha, seasonal specials) over black filter coffee. Purist menus need an accessible on-ramp.
- **Health and wellness.** Interest in functional add-ins, alt-milks, low-sugar options, and matcha.
- **Remote work.** Cafés serve as informal workspaces. That helps dwell-time revenue but hurts table turnover.
- **Input-cost shock.** Arabica and robusta hit record prices in 2024–25. Roasters raised prices, and the gap between specialty and commodity coffee narrowed (see [09-risks.md](09-risks.md)).

## The Miami market specifically

### Population and economy
- Miami-Dade County: **about 2.74M residents** (2024), up **3.7%** since 2020 (Census V2025).
- **70.6% Hispanic or Latino**, **54.5% foreign-born**, and **75.3%** speak a language other than English at home.
- Median household income **$71,753** (ACS 2020–2024), with big gaps between neighborhoods: Brickell, Coral Gables, and Coconut Grove are far higher than the county median.
- Ongoing in-migration of finance and tech workers and remote workers since 2020 has added high-income professionals, concentrated in Brickell, Downtown, Edgewater, and Wynwood.

### Tourism (a second demand engine)
- **28.35M visitors** to Greater Miami & Miami Beach in 2025 (+0.4% year over year, 17.3% above 2019). That includes 6.26M international, 13.08M domestic out-of-state, and 9.01M Florida residents.
- **$22.7B** in direct visitor spending in 2025.
- Top international markets: Colombia, Brazil, UK, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Spain, Italy, Germany, France. Many of these have strong espresso cultures and high quality expectations.
- **Seasonality:** peak season runs from Dec to April (Art Basel in early December, winter snowbirds, spring events). Hotel occupancy drops sharply from July to September (forward bookings around 20–30%). That is also hurricane season.

### Local coffee culture
- Miami has one of the strongest everyday coffee habits in the U.S., built on **Cuban coffee**: the *cafecito*, *colada* (shared, about $1.50–$3.30), *cortadito*, and *café con leche*. It is served from **ventanitas** (walk-up windows) and costs very little.
- The specialty scene took off around **2010** with **Panther Coffee** in Wynwood and has grown since, with local roasters (Panther, Per'La) and national and international brands (La Colombe, Allpress, and others).
- Price ladder today: cafecito/colada **$1.50–$3.50** → specialty pour-over **$5–7** → Wynwood specialty latte **up to about $8**.

## Implications for the concept
1. Demand comes from three sources: **locals' daily habit**, **affluent professionals**, and **tourists**. A site that captures two of the three is much less risky.
2. The menu needs a **cold/iced-first** design and a **Cuban-coffee bridge**, such as a specialty-bean cortadito or colada, to win locals' daily visits.
3. Revenue will be **seasonal**. Model a 15–30% summer dip in tourist-heavy areas (*est.*).
