# 09 — Biggest Risks

Ranked by **likelihood × impact** for a first independent café in Miami. Ratings: H = high, M = medium, L = low.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Why it matters in Miami | Mitigations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Occupancy cost too high for sales volume** | H | H | Asking rents: Brickell $89/sf, Miami Beach $100/sf, Wynwood/DD $71/sf, plus NNN. Overall vacancy is a tight 3.1%, with rents at a record high. A $10k/month rent needs about $100k/month in sales. | Target occupancy ≤10% of sales. Consider corridors at $45–55/sf (Coral Way, Biscayne Corridor, Downtown with 9.6% vacancy). Smaller footprint (700–1,000 sf). Negotiate TI, free rent, a permitting contingency, and percentage-rent caps. |
| 2 | **Permitting and buildout delays and cost overruns** | H | H | Multiple agencies (DBPR, city or county CU, fire, building, DERM). Building permits can take months, and hurricane-code requirements add cost. | Second-generation café space. Zoning check before LOI. Rent starts on permit issuance or opening. Experienced local permit expediter and general contractor. 10–15% contingency. |
| 3 | **Undercapitalization / slow ramp** | M–H | H | Most cafés take 12–24 months to become profitable, and owners underestimate costs by 20–50%. Seasonality adds a summer cash dip. | 4–6 months of working capital. Conservative-case P&L. Pre-sales and memberships. Staged launch (pop-up, then café). |
| 4 | **Hurricanes, flooding, and insurance** | M (yearly) | H | June–November hurricane season. Named-storm deductibles are a percentage of insured value. Some areas are in flood zones. Premiums are above the national average. Closures cause lost revenue and spoiled inventory. | Check FEMA flood zone before signing. Business interruption plus flood coverage. Hurricane shutters or impact glass. Generator/inventory plan. Cloud POS. Cash reserve. |
| 5 | **Labor cost and turnover** | H | M | $15 minimum since 9/30/2026, CPI-indexed after that. Barista pay is $15–23. Housing costs are high. Competition for skilled baristas. | Efficient workflow and equipment, smart scheduling, tip program, career path, cross-training, retention perks. |
| 6 | **Green coffee price volatility and tariffs** | M | M | Arabica hit records in 2025, and U.S. tariffs (up to 50% on Brazil) cut Brazilian imports 46% in Aug 2025 before being lifted in Nov 2025. Arabica was about $2.70/lb in late 2026, down about 32% from a year earlier, but trade policy remains uncertain. | Fixed-price or forward contracts with the roaster. Multiple origins. Menu prices with buffer. Raise the average ticket through food and retail. |
| 7 | **Seasonality** | H | M | Tourism peaks Dec–Apr and drops July–Sept (hotel forward occupancy around 20–30%). Summer heat lowers foot traffic. | Locate where residents are the base. Catering and wholesale revenue. Summer iced-drink promos. Flexible staffing. |
| 8 | **Saturation and competition** | M | M | Wynwood, Brickell, and Miami Beach have many cafés plus luxury entrants (Sant Ambroeus 2026/2028) and chains with apps. | Clear differentiation (specialty Cuban/Latin focus, speed). Underserved corridors. Strong product and hospitality. Map competitors before choosing a site. |
| 9 | **Price sensitivity / cafecito anchor** | M | M | Locals compare to $1.50–$3.30 coladas and may balk at $7–8 lattes. | Value tier (specialty cafecito), loyalty rewards, explain quality without being snobby. |
| 10 | **Parking and access** | M | M | Car-dependent city. Paid or scarce parking in prime areas hurts quick-stop visits. | Prioritize sites with parking, a quick-stop zone, or high walk-by traffic. Pickup and delivery options. |
| 11 | **Regulatory and tax changes** | L–M | M | Local tax differences (resort taxes in Miami Beach; 2% F&B tax if alcohol is served). Sidewalk café rules. Wage indexing. | Compliance calendar. Accountant familiar with Miami-Dade hospitality. |
| 12 | **Climate and utilities** | H | L–M | Year-round heavy air conditioning use, high electricity bills, and humidity that affects coffee storage and equipment. | Efficient HVAC, an air curtain at the entrance, climate-controlled bean storage, preventive maintenance. |

## Top 3 to settle before signing a lease
1. **Site economics:** realistic sales forecast vs. all-in occupancy for each shortlisted site.
2. **Permit path:** zoning by right, second-gen condition, and the timeline to opening.
3. **Capital:** a fully funded budget, including contingency and 4–6 months of runway.

## Open questions
- Run a flood-zone and insurance quote for each shortlisted site.
- Check whether the business plan survives a 25% revenue shortfall plus a 2-week hurricane closure.
