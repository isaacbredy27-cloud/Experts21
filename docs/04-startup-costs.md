## Miami-adjusted budget, 1,000–1,200 sf café (*est.*)

Miami costs higher than national averages: rents of $47–$100/sf, expensive deposits, high construction labor costs, hurricane-code requirements, and permitting time.

**Planning number:** about **$350k** for a lean second-generation café in a non-prime corridor, and **$550k+** for a raw space in Brickell, Wynwood, or Miami Beach.

## Ways to reduce capex
- **Take over a former café** ("second-gen"). This can save $50k–$150k on plumbing, electrical, and hoods.
- **Negotiate tenant-improvement (TI) allowance and free rent.** Easier in submarkets with higher vacancy (Downtown 9.6%, Wynwood/Design District 8.2%, Miami Beach 6.2%). Harder in Coral Gables (1.6%) or Coconut Grove (2.4%).
- **Lease or finance equipment** to preserve cash.
- **Start smaller.** A kiosk, cart, pop-up, or market stall ($25k–$100k) proves the brand before committing to a full lease.
- **Don't roast at first.** Buy wholesale or white-label from a local roaster, and add roasting later (+$50k–$120k plus air permits).

## Funding options
- SBA 7(a) loans and SBA microloans (up to $50k). Lenders generally want a business plan, good personal credit, and 10–30% owner equity.
- Equipment financing and leasing.
- Landlord TI allowance.
- Community or pre-sale funding (prepaid credits / membership models).
- Friends-and-family or angel investment.

## Open questions
- Get 2–3 contractor ballpark quotes for a 1,000 sf café buildout in Miami-Dade.
- Ask equipment dealers (Miami has several La Marzocco/Slayer dealers) for current pricing, lease terms, and service contracts.
