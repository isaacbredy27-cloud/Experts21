# 07 — Staffing

## Wage law (Florida)

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Florida minimum wage from **Sept 30, 2026** | **$15.00/hr** | Fla. Const. Art. X §24 (Amendment 2, 2020); FloridaCommerce |
| Tipped minimum wage (cash wage) | **$11.98/hr** (min wage minus a fixed $3.02 tip credit). Tips must bring the worker to at least $15. | Fla. Const. Art. X §24 |
| Previous rate (9/30/2025–9/29/2026) | $14.00 ($10.98 tipped) | State Attorney notice |
| From Sept 30, 2027 on | Indexed to inflation (CPI) each year | Fla. Stat. 448.110 |

Other employer obligations: federal and state payroll taxes, Florida reemployment tax, **workers' comp** (required at 4+ employees in non-construction industries), I-9, **E-Verify** (Florida requires it for private employers with 25+ employees), mandatory labor-law posters, and federal overtime over 40 hrs/week.

## Team model for a 1,100 sf café, open about 7am–6pm, 7 days (*est.*)

| Role | Headcount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General manager (owner-operator or hired) | 1 | Ordering, scheduling, P&L, hiring. Must hold the Certified Food Protection Manager certificate. |
| Shift leads | 2 | Covers open and close 7 days. |
| Baristas (FT + PT) | 6–8 | Peak: 3 on the floor (bar, register/handoff, support). Off-peak: 2. |
| **Total** | **9–11 people** | About **230–280 labor hours/week** |

Cost check: about 260 hrs × about $17.50 blended wage ≈ $4,550/week ≈ $19.7k/month, plus about 10–12% payroll taxes and workers' comp ≈ **$22k/month**, plus GM salary if not the owner. That is about **30–35% of sales** at $70k/month (see [05](05-operating-costs.md)).

## Hiring considerations specific to Miami
- **Bilingual (English/Spanish) is essential.** Portuguese is a plus in tourist areas (Brazil is the #2 international visitor market).
- There is a real pool of experienced specialty baristas from Panther, La Colombe, Allpress, and others, but competition for them is intense. **Pay above the minimum** plus tips and a training path to retain them.
- Many workers commute by car or bus, so **parking and transit access** matter for retention. Brickell, Downtown, and Wynwood parking is expensive.
- Summer slowdown: plan **cross-training** and reduced hours rather than layoffs. Use the slower season for training and menu development.

## Training and quality
- Pre-opening: 2–4 weeks paid training (budgeted in [04](04-startup-costs.md)).
- Calibration: daily espresso dial-in, weekly cupping, and drink specs with photos.
- SCA (Specialty Coffee Association) barista modules for leads. Food handler certificates for everyone within 60 days of hire.
- Service standards: target ticket times (for example, under 3 minutes for espresso drinks at peak) and mobile-order handoff.

## Tools
- Scheduling and payroll: 7shifts, Homebase, Gusto, or Toast Payroll.
- Tip pooling: must exclude managers and owners (FLSA rules).

## Open questions
- Is the owner going to be the operator (saves a GM salary in Year 1), or will we hire a GM?
- Tip model: tip pool vs. service-included, and the effect on wage costs.
- Benefits strategy (health stipend, free drinks, education) as a retention tool.
